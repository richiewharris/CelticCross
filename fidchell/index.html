Fidchell

The "wood-wisdom" of the early Irish: gold against white on a board of 25 holes. Trap an enemy piece between two of yours to take it.

Opponent

Pieces

How to play

  • Each side has five pieces. Gold moves first.
  • On your turn, move one piece any distance in a straight line, along a row or column, through empty holes. No diagonals, no jumping.
  • Capture by trapping an enemy piece between the piece you just moved and another of yours, in a line. One move can take up to three pieces this way.
  • Moving into a gap between two enemy pieces is safe. You're only taken when they close on you.
  • Win by cutting your rival down to one piece, or by leaving them with no move.
  • After 30 moves in a row without a capture, the side with more pieces wins. Equal pieces is a draw.

Fidchell's rules are lost. Irish texts say its pieces were split into gold and white halves and taken by trapping between two. This version follows that on a compact 5×5 board, marked like the 7×7 Ballinderry board, with moves borrowed from the Roman game it's often compared with, ludus latrunculorum.
