Fidchell of Cups

Fidchell played by the rules of Tablero. Mead against wine on the 7×7 board: two dice move your cups, trap a rival cup between two of yours and drink it, and they refill it from their own jug. Drain their jug to win.

Game

How to play

  • Each side has seven cups on its back row and a jug holding four refills. Mead goes first.
  • Roll both dice. Each die moves a different cup exactly that many holes, in a straight line along a row or column, through empty holes. You choose the cups and the directions.
  • If a move fits a die, you must make it. If neither die fits, your turn ends.
  • A total of 7, 11 or 12 is a pass: your turn ends. Otherwise, keep rolling until you roll a pass or can't move.
  • Trap a rival cup between the cup you moved and another of yours, in a line, and drink it. Moving into a gap between two rival cups is safe.
  • A drunk cup goes back to its owner's back row, refilled from their jug. A player who must refill an empty jug loses.
  • The Queen's number is rolled before play. Roll it and you toast the Queen: drink any rival cup instead of moving, then roll again. Roll again without toasting and that's treason. Your rival drinks one of your cups.
  • If 60 moves pass with no cup drunk, whoever has drunk more wins.

Built from Fidchell's capture-by-trapping and Tablero's board, dice, pass numbers, Queen's toast and refills. A win where your jug was never touched is a skunk.
