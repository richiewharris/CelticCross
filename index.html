Celtic Cross Games

Small games to play in the browser: a lost strategy game brought back, the board game of early Ireland, a puzzle lock carved in stone, and a dice race to twenty. Nothing to install.

Strategy · 2 players or vs computer

Monsiv

A fast cousin of chess on a 5×5 board. Each side has a Throne, two Angels and two Crosses, and every piece steps one square at a time. The twist is the swap: trade places with a piece of your own instead of moving, and catch your opponent off guard. Take the enemy Throne or reach the far row to win.

  • 5×5 board
  • 5 pieces a side
  • Games in 5–10 minutes

Strategy · 2 players or vs computer

Fidchell

The "wood-wisdom" of early Ireland. Gold against white on a pegged board: slide your pieces along rows and columns and trap an enemy piece between two of yours to take it. Its rules were lost centuries ago. This version follows what the old Irish texts say about the game.

  • 5×5 board
  • Capture by trapping
  • Hints for new players

Puzzle · solo

Celtic Cross Lock

Pick the lock on a Celtic puzzle box. Turn the stones of a carved dial until its bronze knot runs unbroken, with only a third of the dial showing through the shield at a time. Press the triskele to try the lock: get it right and the pins drop home and the dial spins free.

  • 5 lock tiers
  • Moves set by your skill
  • Cheat mode to learn

Dice · 2 players or vs computer

Fiche

A race to twenty with three odd dice: a bone caltrop, a wire-wrapped cube, and two cubes on a rod that only shows a 1 when it lands standing on end. Take turns rolling into one shared total. A three or a seven rolls again, nine hands the round to your rival, twelve is a draw, and only an exact twenty wins.

  • 3 dice each
  • Best of 3 or 5
  • Coach shows your odds
